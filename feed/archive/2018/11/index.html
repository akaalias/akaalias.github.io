How To Discover What You Really Need the Most With the \"Needs Card Deck\"

I totally forgot to mention this, but I made a card deck last year that has helped me explore and discover needs as part of my personal development.

I’ve used the deck to rapidly and easily identify and understand those parts of my life that were either helping me move forward or that were holding me back emotionally.

It’s also a radically insightful tool for…

  • Relationship Development
  • Customer Needs Research
  • Design Research

Why Use Needs As a Starting Point For Personal Development?

A major reason to use these “Needs” as my starting point for personal investigations was that, according to one school of thought, emotions (positive and negative ones) are always the result of an important need that’s either met (positive feelings) or unmet (negative feelings.)

Universal Needs, Individual Responses

Furthermore, while needs are relatively universal, the individual response to them are highly individual. I find this deeply fascinating.

For example, if I’m currently in need of community and that need is met, I may feel positively calm.

Someone else may feel positively excited for the same reason.

If my need for community isn’t met, I may feel negatively frustrated while someone else may feel negatively bored:

So, to better understand my emotions (and my actions that come from them) I use the inventory of needs to navigate an otherwise complex ball of golden twine also know as my psyche.

There are a bunch of ways to use the cards, but I’ve found two that work the best for me.

Strategy 1: Drive By Intuition

The first one is simply to spread out the cards unsorted and start to pick out those cards that I intuitively respond to. Once I’ve found a handful or so needs that I’m interested in, I start to discuss them with a partner or coach or simply with myself.

The main questions then are:

  • Is this a met or unmet need?
  • How does it make me feel?
  • If it’s an unmet need, what can I do to change that?

The last question can quickly drive out an action I can take today to make my life better.

Strategy #2: Dump and Sort

The second strategy is even simpler and just as effective to get to a selection of met, unmet and irrelevant needs:

For each card, I ask myself:

  • Is this an important, met need?
  • Is this an important, unmet need?
  • Is this an irrelevant need at the moment?

Going through each card, I wind up with three stacks: MET, UNMET and IRRELEVANT.

I then focus on the UNMET needs again because they contribute to my feeling the most unease and devise action items to solve them.

Needs are Values

I never really connected with when people, and more often, companies, talked what their values. It just seemed so abstract. Co-incidentally, discovering important needs also becomes the list of my personal values. Things that I value above all else.

Wednesday – 14.11.2018

Wednesday afternoon, at home, not crazy about being here but I think I’m dragging out the cold I got over a week ago, so I took a nap just now and feel better. Didn’t feel up to bar at all this morning and still went on a nice bike-ride with my dad. We visited Factory which I decided isn’t a place that I want to go to anymore. 

I also realized, based on my father’s observation, that the vibe of people sitting alongside long tables in silence at their computers totally feels like a factory. That they look like factory workers, quietly typing along and not talking to each other. 

I guess that’s one of the reasons I’ve felt so alienated there for so long. I just couldn’t put my finger on it. 

So, I think it’ll be best to cancel my subscription this week and avoid paying another $130 per month for a place that I don’t enjoy. I’ll be able to put that money towards things that give me joy and move me forward. It’s kinda sad because the Factory building is really nice. But I also realized that the endless amount of heavy doors you have to open and close to get from A to B really disrupts my flow of thinking and working. 

I also think I will cancel my Drip.com subscription and the Javelin.com subscription, too. In both cases I can use other, cheaper services. I will keep my Clickfunnels subscription because that’s been really working well. It’s a good tool and I’m learning a lot with it. For Drip I can use Mailchimp for which I still have an account. For Javelin I’m already using Evernote instead.

Do I have to decide between \"anything goes\" and a \"specific topic\" for my blog?

So, I’ve been pretty good at publishing at least one post each week for the past two months. I’m proud of having published a bunch of posts that I hope someone in the future will find useful. 

But here’s the rub: Right now, I’m just writing “anything goes” style, meaning that whatever happens to be interesting to ME that day, I write about it. 

Now, that’s a lot of fun, but I’m questioning if I’m making the best use of my time this way. Right now, I don’t have a specific audience with this blog. Maybe my friends and family who, once in a while check it out to get a sense of what’s on my mind. I guess that is an audience, but I also speak with them on the phone. 

I’m really wondering if I should write and publish…

…more deliberately – on a specific topic for a specific audience– or just for myself as I have. I’ve been racking my brain for a week now and have not come to a decision. 

Well, actually…

What I have come to as a decision is that what matters to me right now even more is to increase the regularity first. That was an initial goal (to publish something of value every day) and that has suffered because I haven’t formed a solid routine yet. 

Some days I might be in the position to post something. Some days I might not. (For days where I don’t have wifi, that’s okay, I can still write it in Evernote)

Anyways, so I just realized that, while I don’t know yet “where” my blogging is going – I can’t seem to make up my mind –, I do know that it needs to be more regular, daily. 

For me, developing a daily creative practice is more important than knowing exactly what to write about. 

In fact, I have a feeling that with more practice the direction will start to emerge.

Thanks for reading!

Alexis

How I’ve Rebuilt the Giraffe App with TDD and Speed In Mind

Background: As you may have seen, I built myself a goal-setting, planning and execution app that helps me to consistently, every day, work towards my dreams. After reading several motivational books, they usually would boil down to the advice to “Set a goal, make a plan and make progress every single day.” I think that’s solid advice.

With that in mind, I tried to follow this advice but I couldn’t find a tool that would allow me to do it all in one place. I tried every task manager and todo list out there, but none allowed me to (inclusively)

  • Set high-level goals
  • Plan, break down and prioritize those goals into tiny, bite-sized tasks and
  • Execute on a simple todo list that’s auto-generated based on my planning, break down and prioritization

For years, I had tried to represent this flow with apps like Evernote and Google Docs (for writing down my goals), Omni Outliner (for breaking down and prioritizing the tasks needed) and Trello (for keeping an active backlog that I could work off of.) But nothing was integrated.

So, two months ago, I knew exactly what I wanted and built a simple Rails web app that gave me that flow. The app itself worked but it wasn’t particularly well-built. My biggest question, the reason I built it, was “Will this actually help me be more motivated and productive?”

I started to use my app for 6 weeks. It did precisely what I wanted it to and I concluded two weeks ago, that this wasn’t just a nice idea but a valuable tool.

Now, as I had added new goals and broken them down into easy, 5-minute tasks, my database had grown to 300+ nested tasks.

That’s when the app got slow. To the point where I couldn’t use it anymore without feeling frustrated again. (Because to me, Flow is everything.)

I figured this is the right time to throw away the prototype and start building a “real” piece of production software that has:

  • Complete feature-parity and look-and-feel to the prototype
  • Full automated test-coverage for all features
  • A significant speed-up even with hundreds or thousands of sub-tasks

The list of features from the prototype became my backlog so it would be the “same app” overall. Since the prototype had gone through several iterations already, I knew exactly what I wanted.

I decided to write feature-level automated tests because I think they’re the highest level of quality assurance that makes sense at this stage (I still might throw the app out if it doesn’t work for me down the line.)

To give you a sense, here is the ‘Completing a task’ feature-test story in prose and then in code:

  • Given I am on the execute tab
  • And I have an un-started task
  • When I click start
  • Then I see the button complete

…becomes..

I experimented with different performance improvements and landed on storing the “task tree” (the slowest piece in the prototype) directly in the database, as a binary object using Ruby’s Marshal.load and Marshal.dump. Here is an example of what I’m talking about.

Overall the development experience has been great. I use strict outside-in TDD/BDD, where I

  • Write the test based on the “Given, When, Then” prose from the backlog.
  • Run the test and see it fail
  • Fix exactly the reason the test failed
  • Rinse and repeat until the feature is done and the test passes

Anyways, after a few evenings, the app has now MVP/prototype feature-parity and I’m using it every day. It’s fast. It’s a bit nicer now and maintainable because of the tests.
