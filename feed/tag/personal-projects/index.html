Mosey: Walks that start themselves — the world's longest-lived people don't exercise. They simply get up and move every 20 minutes.

The longest-lived people don’t exercise — they get up and move every twenty minutes. I built Mosey to put that nudge into a Mac workday: after twenty minutes at the desk, your walking pad starts on its own, three to twenty minutes, a different walk each time at a pace it sets. Step on when you’re ready, step off whenever you like. Nothing counts down at you, no streaks, no account — and it’s free. Coming to the Mac App Store.

Quasi: "Say it, hear it done" — an experiment in computing without a screen

We spend ten or more hours a day staring at screens. Quasi is my small experiment in computing without one: you speak into a pocket recorder while your phone stays locked in your bag, and a few short sounds tell you that you were heard, understood, and that the thing you asked for is done. An aural language for a screen-free life — an experiment, not a product.

Human as Sensor: Call-Based Reporting for C2 | European Defense Tech Hackathon – Berlin 2026

Our project at the European Defense Tech Hackathon in Berlin, July 2026, in the words of my teammate Won YI:

Together with Eugen Leikom from strixx.ai and Alexis Rondeau, we built “Human as Sensor”, a lightweight call-based reporting and intake prototype.

The idea was simple. A civilian or service member reports an incident through an ordinary phone call. Calls can be handled in DE, EN and other major languages, then converted into structured text and data for a C2 environment.

The phone-call format was deliberate. In degraded conditions, basic voice may remain usable when apps or higher-bandwidth services are unavailable. Where available, GSM/2G can provide an additional fallback. The same interface becomes usable once basic civilian voice service returns, without a new app, device or additional training.

— Won YI
