pudding-app

A better way to show you did the work

“PS: I wrote this reply 100% by hand.”*

In an age of AI content, Pudding lets you prove you’re human and that you put in the work.

No AI hesitates. No AI deletes three versions of a sentence. No AI stares at a blank cursor for 30 seconds. But you do. And that’s what makes your work human and unique.

Pudding: Where the proof is

Show the work you put in

I kept adding “P.S.: This was 100% hand-written by me without AI” to everything I published. But who believes that anymore? Everyone says they didn’t use AI. I needed a way to actually prove it—to show my messy, human writing process. So I built Pudding.

Now you can PROVE your writing is human.

Pudding records every keystroke, every deletion, every moment you pause searching for the right word. All the messy, beautiful evidence that a real human wrote your work.

What you can do

  • Record your entire writing process automatically
  • Jump to any moment with the timeline scrubber
  • Share .pudding files as proof of authenticity
  • Export your writing process to show your work

No AI hesitates. No AI deletes three versions of a sentence. No AI stares at a blank cursor for 30 seconds. But you do. And now you can prove it.

Perfect if you’re

  • A writer tired of being accused of using AI
  • A student proving academic integrity
  • A freelancer building client trust
  • A content creator showing authentic work
  • Anyone who wants to say “watch me write this”

Your words. Your process. Your proof.

Privacy First

Pudding is completely local. Your documents never leave your Mac. We don’t collect any data, use analytics, or require an internet connection. Read our full Privacy Policy.

System Requirements

  • macOS 14.0 or later
  • Apple Silicon or Intel processor

Hey all! I wanted to share a kind of new development process I’ve been using lately — I call it "Moonwalk."

Basically it's:

  • Check out a spike branch (git checkout -b reconciliation/spike/mt940)
  • Vibe code fast (and with vim and vigour!) until it feels just right
  • Generate a spec based on the final version
  • Switch back to main (git checkout main)
  • Check out only the spec (git checkout reconciliation/spike/mt940 -- /job-stories) <- This is the moonwalk!
  • Let the "professional" agents and sub-agents write production-grade code based on the spec

It's so effective because I can leverage Claude Code for both: Quickly build a working vibe-heavy prototype spec AND write production-grade code with agentic/sub-agentic quality.

For the vibe code part, the standard Claude Code top-level agent is more than sufficient. It's great, no need for time-costly delegation to sub-agents here.

For the production-grade part, since we're an XP shop with a pretty solid TDD process, sub agents make total sense. We turned our existing process and skills into a bunch of sub-agents that work smoothly together:

We've got...

  • green-minimal-implementer(.)md
  • code-quality-reviewer(.)md
  • code-refactorer(.)md
  • db-migration-specialist(.)md
  • dependency-manager(.)md
  • frontend-design-specialist(.)md
  • locale-translation-specialist(.)md
  • red-test-writer(.)md

So, yeah, that's what I wanted to share with you. Try it out and let me know how I can help :)

Cheers!

PS: For better or worse, this post is 100% written by hand
PPS: My friend Alex Fedorov reminded me that this is pretty close to the spike/exploration workflow we used to do at Pivotal Labs. I think he's right.

cc'ing @claudeai and @Lovable because I feel like it's a nice connection between the two of you <3

We're a classic XP shop. To build new features in our brown-field app, we defined about 8 sub-agents such as "red-test-writer", "minimal-green-implementer" and "refactorer".

Claude Code sub-agents are now fully integrated in our day-to-day workflow.

I think two reasons it's going great for us:

1. The main agent loop plans and then autonomously calls them up depending on the current context. The convenience to not having to remember to do this manually is crucial.
2. As an XP shop, we always had a few very clearly defined roles, like "Red/Green Test Writer", "Code Refactorer" and "Test Coverage Analyzer" and "Code Quality Reviewer" - which were easy to model as sub-agents and describe when to call them up.

tldr; @lovable helped her build an app. It helped me find compassion.

This week, my wife built her first app using Lovable. She finally felt what it’s like to get lost in the bliss of creating digital products, and I finally felt what it’s like to be outside of a loved one’s creative process.

For context:

My wife is an executive leadership coach. Her work is in-person, relationship-focused, and non-technical.

I’m a software engineer with 25 years of experience, and for me, making new things has always meant quality time with myself and learning.

But… The love for making things also put pressure on our relationship:

When I got absorbed in coding personal projects, I would vanish for hours, often days, hyper-focused, even thinking about the problem when off-screen.

So, even though I was physically “there,” my wife experienced absence.

She couldn’t understand why I poured so much energy into these side projects. And then my attempts to show her “the latest version” usually made things worse for her: too excited, too technical, too too hard to follow. And then, of course, I would feel misunderstood and alienated in turn. Yeah.

This week, something shifted. For the first time, my wife built her own app—with Lovable. She got hooked. She disappeared for hours, making, testing, playing around, chasing down bugs, refining features, obsessing over details.

Two days ago she sent me this:

“[…] since yesterday, the automatic cataloging of the photos and the recommendation engine have already been fundamentally improved, but right now the database integration is a bit buggy.”

I couldn’t believe it. She had stepped into my world of making. And I had stepped into her world of waiting, standing outside, feeling the distance, rooting for her and at the same time wondering when she’d come back.

That role reversal gave us what years of explaining never could: perspective and empathy.

She now knows what it feels like to lose yourself in digital creation. I now know what it feels like to be left “outside” of it.

Yesterday and today we’ve reflected on this change in perspectives. I feel much more understood in my love for making and I feel a deeper understanding for how my deep-dives felt for her.

Thank you Lovable!

PS: One a side-note, I’m also in awe. In just three days, she fully iterated on her idea and built a solid product with actual non-trivial technology. I say this as someone who’s been doing this for 25 years: her app is getting really, really good.

@lovable PPS: Bumping in hope to hear if others have had similar issues or experiences regarding pre- and post-AI vibe-coding deep dives. @lovable has this come up at all on your radar?
