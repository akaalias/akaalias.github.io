New Retrospective Format: \"Highlights, Blessings and Successes\"

I’ve created an empowering and fun new Retrospective format for myself. I’ve enjoyed it tremendously.

I’ve found that the more I focus on the good that’s happening in my life, the better I feel.

I call the format “Highlights, Blessings and Successes.”

As you probably guessed it, you collect and appreciate exactly the things that went well.

  • Highlights – For me, that’s usually unexpectedly cool things that happened either with or without my doing.
  • Blessings – For me, that’s usually something I take for granted but that makes my life so much easier.
  • Successes – For me, that’s usually something I did and that I completed.

In your day, week, month or year.

It’s super easy and I want you to try it.

Make an (unordered) list of all the things that were good. You don’t have to know yet if they’re a highlight, blessing or success. Just make a list and when you go through it again, you can mentally or with a pen categorize each item.

It’s really simple and really fun. I have even started to just mentally go through the day and recap and think about “What were my highlights?”, “What am I blessed with?”, “How did I succeed today?”

Give it a try and see what happens!

Confidence Is Everything: Paul Graham's Startup Curve and the Dunning-Kruger Effect

Yesterday, I was reading in Grant Cardone’s book “Sell Or Be Sold” and got stuck on the idea that “To the degree that you’re sold yourself on the product, idea, company, service, support you will sell to others. If you’re not 100% sold yourself, you can’t sell to others.”

Could the story of your confidence in your vision be expressed in both, “The Startup Curve” and the Dunning-Kruger effect? Are they potentially the same?

Paul Graham and Trevor Blackwell beautifully and cheekily captured this in “The Startup Curve” sometime around 2008.

What struck me is, how much it reminds me of the Dunning-Kruger effect. I think there’s a correlation because, in the end, what sells and makes a company move forward is utter conviction. That type of motivation is abundant in the beginning but, as we learn more, we get our slices of humble-pie and have to wade through ’the trough of sorrow’ aka. ‘Valley of despair’ until we’re ready to gain wisdom to once again climb the ladder of success.

Experiment: I'm Shutting Down Internet Access at Home. Again.

3 months ago I canceled my internet service at home for two reasons:

  1. The service provider sucked and
  2. I wanted to spend less time online and be less distracted

So, for two months I lived without high-speed internet at home.

I read 9 books.

I slept better because no more Netflix or Youtube before bed.

I generally planned my media/information consumption better.

I liked it very much.

I felt freed and less distracted.

But, a month ago I started working on a project with a company in the US.

The time-difference means the time I can interact with the team is from 3pm to 7pm CET.

At that time it became awkward to find a spot to be online, like a cafe or going to the Factory co-working space.

So, I asked my neighbor if she would be so kind to access to her network.\

She did (thank you very much!) and for the last 3 weeks I’ve been online at home again.

Needless to say, I got sucked back the old habits.

I again checked news, reddit, youtube and wasted hours on Netflix…

So, last night I made a pro/cons list and decided to go offline again.  

But with a twist… It’s not black and white:

  • I need to access work stuff on my laptop. I have kept the network on my laptop.
  • I like having Alexa there for music. Alexa’ll stay online as well.
  • I wasted too much time on my phone. I removed the network from my phone.
  • I wasted far too much time on my iPad. I removed the network from my iPad.

I cut the cords on my phone and iPad yesterday afternoon. 

And I already spent the evening reading again and slept better.

This Week's Challenge: “Only positive thoughts and actions for 24h“

In Grant Cardone’s book “Sell Or Be Sold”, he challenges the reader to start a timer and aim to only think and act positively for 24 hours.

There is only one rule:

When a negative, self-limiting belief or action happens, you have to reset the timer and start over.

I’ve challenged myself for a 4 days now.

I’ve “failed” at least 10 times but it’s getting easier and more fun with practice.

Today I finally completed a full 24 hours!

What I like about it:

  • I enjoy a good challenge.
  • It’s a lot of fun.
  • It’s deeply personal.
  • It has an uplifting, empowering effect on my attitude and outlook.
  • I’ve become more aware of “head trash” that otherwise sneaks into my head unnoticed.

Plus, It’s easy to try (and no one needs to know.)

All you have to do is simply…

  • Open your phone’s clock app
  • Set a timer to 24 hours
  • Start the timer and focus on positive thoughts and actions
  • And… if you slip up, reset the timer.

Community Trend Watch: Shortlist Berlin December 2018

For Market Researchers: Social Trends is a free tool that lets you stay ahead of the curve by giving you the fastest growing Meetups in the ‘taste-maker’ cities of New York, Berlin, London and San Francisco.

Trend Watch Focus Berlin

There is a ton of activity in Berlin, Meetups are happening left and right. Over the course of the last year, I’ve seen oodles of fitness, social entrepreneurship and Blockchain-related Meetups come and go.

But today, I would like to share with you a shortlist of interesting communities that have shown consistent growth and strong “gravity” attracting high numbers of members.

Meetup.ai

Started only 8 months ago, Meetup.ai is a community built around Artificial Intelligence. With a consistent growth rate it has attracted 1,422 members to date.

From the founders:

Witness AI in Action
We believe in creating opportunities and connecting people with technology that goes beyond theoretical knowledge to real world applications. We believe in challenging the status quo and the unlimited power of technology to lift society into the next era

Professional Network
We invite everybody to an exchange of knowledge and offer an open environment for people to connect, find inspiration and imagination. We are shaping a community of exceptional personalities that has the sheer potential to push the limits beyond imaginable achievements

Conferences, Meetups, Workshops
We are hosting these kind of events, enabling people to enhance their personal understanding for the realistic applications of Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning and other disruptive technologies that will shape the world of tomorrow. We are providing resources and bringing great speakers to Hamburg for them to share their fascinating stories and unique insights from real world projects

Naturally, Artificial Intelligence is experiencing a huge lift over the past two years. Oddly, I haven’t seen as many growing Meetups around this topic in Berlin. Meetup.ai clearly has put in the work and that’s why I think you should keep an eye on them moving forward.

Zero Waste Berlin

A beautiful social movement to reduce waste to zero is a Meetup perfectly suited to Berlin. While “only” 257 members as of today, the constant growth, regardless of seasons, shows how it is a consistent concern to people in Berlin.

If you look at the curve below, you’ll see very smooth and straight growth which makes me think it will continue to grow organically for the coming year. And with that I expect to hear more about Zero Waste in the future!

Token Engineering

The Berlin Token Engineering Meetup is another high-growth community that has attracted over 500 members in the past 6 months. Overall, a nice growth curve with an acceleration between June and September with an otherwise constant trajectory.

With more than a few hundred Blockchain Meetups in Berlin alone, this one stands out by having grown to 500 members around the very technical aspect of the field of token engineering. With this kind of growth I expect this community to continue to thrive and prosper.

From the founders:

Come join us and plant seeds for an active and intelligent community around which we can build a new engineering field - Token Engineering.

HumanCharger Unboxing: First Impressions and Use

My HumanCharger device is finally here, after a long, long, long, long wait.

I’m very excited and happy!

Here are a few things I noticed:

Unboxing and First Impressions:

  • Nice, clean packaging
  • Easy to open
  • Was able to turn it on right away
  • The device itself is made of smooth metal and feels nice
  • The earplugs seem standard, made of plastic and rubber
  • The LEDs are smaller (2mm diameter) than I expected and as bright as I can imagine they get (not blindingly bright though)
  • There are bright LEDs in the ear-plugs and not “fiber-optic cable endpoints” as Oleksii and I had theorized for heat-management.

  • Technical specifications from the booklet:

    • Typical session duration: 12 minutes
    • Average light intensity: > 10,000 lux (within 1cm of the LED)
    • Peak wavelength: 450nm

First Use

  • The earplugs fit comfortably in my ears though I’m not sure if they’re positioned correctly. No guidance found in the booklet.
  • The earplugs got warm but not uncomfortably so.
  • The battery was empty less than 10 minutes after start. I think that it was low on power to begin with, so I’m charging it now. That makes sense, I didn’t expect it to be 100% charged.

So, right now I’m waiting for a re-charge and the experiment will begin by tomorrow morning. I’ll probably write a few more posts on the progress I’m making with the HumanCharger.

Why I’m a Slow Learner But Fast Performer

Over the last year I went on a deep-dive to learn a new skill: How to market and, by extension, how to sell products that I love.

I’ve enjoyed the learning-process. But to be honest, I’m frustrated because my progress has also been slow.

Well, the good news is… I’m finally “getting there” to a point where I can finally perform.

But, maaaaannn, this took much longer than I expected.

This made me think about how I absorb new things…

First off, I’m a total “single-tasker.”

Yet, once I’ve absorbed it, I can speed up execution and perform fast. Over and over again.

I want to believe that – while my enthusiasm goes full Dunning-Kruger – my performance compounds over time.

I see others breeze through what takes me days, weeks and months. It’s hard not to feel down sometimes.

My “slow but steady” learning style has been my mode of operation since University. Instead of taking all required courses, I usually took half the amount.

While half as fast as others, I was usually top of my class in each of the courses I did take.

Well… when I’m learning something new, I can tell that my brain is completely focused and engaged. I can’t think of anything else for that period.

It’s like when you learned to ride a bike.

In the beginning it was hard and slow. You gotta keep your balance, move your feet, steer with your hands.

It required full attention and time to practice and “sink in.”

Then, with a little bit of consistent practice it got easier and required less attention. To the point where you don’t have to think about it but do.

So, long story short. I’ve learned a lot this year. It took me much longer than I expected.

It’s been very frustrating…

But I’m also noticing how it’s becoming easier and easier every day.
