YO! I just completed my fourth @karpathy autoresearch-inspired project called "Beeline". It's sort of a prototype for "GPS without GPS" for UAVs.

Claude and I iterated through 81 ideas and found a small neural thingy that can be trained to recall 96% of locations, given satellite images to train on and then a camera frame to remember latitude and longitude for.

Basically:
1. Train: Here are all tiles and their latitude/longitude, remember them
2. Infer: Here's a live frame from my camera. Where am I?

Claude for experiment design and implementation, me for research cybernetics.

My favorite part is that the resulting artifact (the .onnx model that represents the memory of spot and lat/lon) is intentionally custom-built for the defined geo-fence and thus the research harness had to embrace overfitting for this one specific case as a feature, not a bug.

The biggest suprise was that in order to break-through the 75-long experiment plateau, I had to fix the harness, not the loop.

And of course, this is a personal research project, so a bunch of limits apply. Such as training only happened on day-time pictures and the training set is bound to a geo-fence and more.

But hey! It's invented and built completely from scratch, so no dependencies on domain-libraries which means it's all GPL-licensed, too!

If you're interested, check it out here! :D

Much to say, maybe in a series of tweets in the coming week. I'll post those below as they roll in.

Continued thoughts/impressions and the like:

I've re-read R.A.W. & R.S.'s "Illumiatus!" trilogy five or six times over the years.

So reading IJ feels like finding a distant cousin in spirit. They're similarly encyclopedic (I've collected the full list of band names from the Lake Totenkopf festival of anyone is interested!), they both switch between POVs (present/past, inner/outer monologues, etc.), they both contain full vaguely related storylines where some cross paths only on the final pages, they both take place in imagined, remembered, hallucinated and "real" situations, they both revolve around odd public and secret organizations, they both contain oddly gratuitous "Mord und Totschlag" and more...

In my opinion, one of the biggest differences I found was that the trilogy goes into action right away while I feel like I've spent 500 pages in IJ carefully observing the status quo and getting a deep, deep, DEEP brief into "But and this is how we got here..." (BTW I love DFW's sentences starting with "But so/and...")

I'm at 65%, discounting the appendix, and I would say, the first actual action started exactly at the 50% mark. The unfolding of the Eschaton game chaos felt like a "OMG finally something is actually happening!".

And, well, if manifesting the "Eschaton" on a tennis court is not a nod to the trilogy, I don't know what is.

Now, one of the ways, that IJ is gaining a very special place in my heart is that I've never read an author with DFW's level of "Aufnahmevermögen", meaning his ability to observe and verbalize all kinds of inner thoughts that, once he's written them down, made me think "Oh, shit. I've had that exact thought or feeling dozens of times but didn't even notice them!"

Currently it's those incredibly microscopic bylines that have kept me coming back most. I'll find and share some examples later.
