The value of g (gravity) varies according to a number of factors: it is greater at Earth’s poles than at the equator, lower at high altitudes than at sea level, and it varies very slightly according to local geology, for example if there are particularly dense rocks near Earth’s surface. If the constant acceleration of an object in free fall near Earth’s surface is represented by g, the height at which it is released is z0 and time is t, then at any stage in its descent, the height of the body above the surface z = z0 – 1⁄2 gt2, where gt is the speed of the body and g its acceleration.
