Bookmarked from Erwin @Erwin_AI

The indie hacking community is turning into some weird bubble where some 'successful' people sell to those that aspire the same success.

Courses, boilerplates, coaching, products targeting other founders..

Entrepreneurship is not a 'follow this checklist' type of thing, but some of these indie hacker influencers make it look like that.

You can identify these tools/products/content bundles by looking at their founder here on Twitter. Are they continuously promoting success and selling a dream only if you buy that product? Is their success the exact thing they're trying to sell you?

'I make 50k a month and so can you with <insert boilerplate/course>, it's what I'm using myself!' > except that the 'success' and 50k a month income comes from selling exactly that.

Products solving real problems don't have a founder that is continuously promising on Twitter how good it is and how bad you need it for your own success. Instead, the product speaks for itself.

Don't read next bit if you're too sensitive (you'll read it anyway hehe):

They're selling tools/info you think you need for a dream you will never achieve unless you are comfortable figuring it out on your own.

If you continuously look for 'quick ways' to do something and buy product after product from these 'successful' indie hacker influencers, hoping to emulate what success they have, you likely won't succeed.

Identifying as (only) an indie hacker, building a product for other founders and only doing your 'marketing' on twitter and product hunt is essentially a limiting belief. You're sticking to a comfortable bubble.

Think bigger, don't be too influenced by this indie hacker / founder bubble and don't fall for the buy-this-product-for-success shit.
