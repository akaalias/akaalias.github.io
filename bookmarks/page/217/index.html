Bookmarked from Startup Archive @StartupArchive_

Steve Jobs on leadership

In the clip below, Steve argues:

“The greatest people are self-managing. They don’t need to be managed. Once they know what to do, they’ll go figure out how to do it… What they need is a common vision, and that’s what leadership is. Leadership is having a vision, being able to articulate that so the people around you can understand it, and getting consensus on a common vision.”

Borrowing from his tagline for the original Mac, Steve continues:

“we wanted people who were insanely great at what they did… and the neatest thing that happens when you get a core group of ten great people is that it becomes self-policing as to who they let into that group. So I consider the most important job of someone like myself to be recruiting.”

This observation on the importance of getting the first ten people right is a refrain you constantly hear from the very best founders. As Patrick Collison put it:

“A large part of the reason why the first ten people you hire are so important is because you’re not just hiring those first ten people. You’re actually hiring 100 people, because you should think of each of those people as bring along another ten people with them.”

The best leaders recruit “insanely great” people and align them around a common vision.

Bookmarked from David Perell @david_perell

It's the creator's job to hide complexity from the consumer.

Take writing. As a writer, your task is to simplify and distill your message until you're left with only the essence of what you're trying to say.

The reader's experience should be frictionless. The sentences should flow. The structure should be intuitive. Getting there requires a lot of editing, deleting, and rearranging that the reader will never appreciate — and that's the point.

This leads to the Paradox of Creativity: Your work is done when it looks so simple that the consumer thinks they could've done it, which means they won't appreciate how hard you worked.

It's been a big week for Open Source AI, and here's one more to cap the week off!

Introducing HelixNet.

HelixNet is a novel Deep Learning architecture consisting of 3 x Mistral-7B LLMs. It has an actor, a critic, and a regenerator.

HelixNet is insprired from an actor-critic architecture most prominent in Reinforcement Learning algorithms. The name derives from Helix, referring to the spiral structure of a DNA molecule. It symbolizes the intertwined nature of the three networks, working in tandem, much like the strands of a DNA molecule.

HelixNet regenerates very pleasing and accurate responses, due to the entropy preservation of the regenerator. Further, in testing, the critic and the regenerator seems readily transferrable to other LLMs.

Bookmarked from Nick @nickbakeddesign

Back in July, we were struggling to find leads who could pay more than $350 for design and now we are at $65,000+ MRR just for baked.design in 3 months.

What has changed?

Start low & grow incrementally:
When we started getbaked, our focus was indie hackers and we knew our growth would be limited due to the audience selection. In late July we entirely stopped offering low-cost design services and started focusing on sharing design breakdowns and roasting.

Roasting:
Yes, roasting! We went back to our roots which triggered all of this you're seeing today. The first roast right after ditching getbaked reached 110k impressions and we got our first organic lead. Someone in the comments suggested building a mini service around tiny UI components, which led to the launching tinyuis. com and this post reached 200k+ impressions and got our first $4,000/monthly retainer customer.

Design process breakdown:
We then started posting thorough design breakdowns on how to design landing pages and web applications. These posts helped prospect leads understand how we work and our design process way better than our portfolio could ever showcase. Again, 80% of these posts gained over 100k impressions each and reached an audience beyond indie hackers. This is when the organic lead flood door got opened, we took calls across two timezones to cater to different clients and at its peak we were talking 8-10 calls a day.

Organic growth and leads:
Instead of cold DMs, emails or promoting baked.design in comments, we choose to go with "Show & tell" strategy which worked really when I launched getbaked back in Mar/Apr and now with baked design at the end of July. We went with showing our work where our prospective lead would be, instead of directing people to our website. Authority in the domain and authenticity were the other two factors which led to immense organic growth and lead generations over the last couple of months. This process led to a 102% increase in overall MRR across movevirtual & baked just in October.

Website & work:
Alex is the sole reason we have a website for baked.design. I never touched the website other than making changes to the Stripe links when we increase prices or copy changes. She built the entire website in days and we launched in late July and got our first monthly retainer on the 26th of July. X is our lead gen & our website is how we convert them. We use @calcom to manage all the meetings (ditched Calendly) and people love it so far, other than the occasional screen-sharing issues and spamming.

It's still just 2 of us running the show, while we look to hire designers to expand and grow baked.design. Thank you indie hacking community for accepting me and helping me grow for the past 9 months.
