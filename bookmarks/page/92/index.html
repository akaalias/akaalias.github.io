Bookmarked from CJ Zafir @cjzafir

Have you fine-tuned any AI model?

I know a few guys that are making 6-figures per month,

Just by providing fine-tuned GPT-4o models to companies.

Best part: NO Coding skills required! as OpenAI provided a proper interface to fine-tune its models.

Here's a guide for you how you can fine-tune a GPT model:

✅️ Step 1: Prepare Your Training Data

Collect Data: Gather input and output data from the company.

Provide questions and answers in pairs as you want the model to respond:

For example:

Input: "What are your business hours?"

Output: "We are open from 9 AM to 5 PM, Monday to Friday."

Format Your Data: Save your examples in a JSON Lines file (.jsonl). Each line should have a prompt (input) and a completion (output), like this:

{"prompt": "What is your refund policy?", "completion": "You can request a refund within 30 days of purchase."}

Tip: Use JSON editor tools to transform text data into .jsonl data.

✅️ Step 2: Log into OpenAI

Go to OpenAI’s platform. (Not ChatGPT but Dev dashboard)

✅️ Step 3: Upload Your Training File

Navigate to Fine-Tuning Section in the left sidebar.

Upload Data:

Click the "Upload File" button and select your .jsonl file.

✅️ Step 4: Start a Fine-Tuning Job

Create a Job:

After uploading your file, click Create Fine-Tune.

Select the base model (e.g., gpt-4o, gpt-4o-mini) to start fine-tuning.

Configure Settings:

You can add optional parameters like validation files or adjust the learning rate, but defaults work for most cases.

Start Fine-Tuning:

Click “Start Job.” The process may take some time depending on your dataset size.

You can notice good results from 2M to 5M token dataset. (Also, the quality of dataset matters)

✅️ Step 5: Monitor the Progress

Check Status:

The fine-tuning dashboard will show the progress of your job.

Once completed, you’ll see a new fine-tuned model ID.

✅️ Step 6: Use Your Fine-Tuned Model

Use the new model ID in your API calls to generate responses.

For example, specify your model in the API like this:

{"model": "fine-tuned-model-id", "prompt": "You are a Customer Support Expert"}

Test It:

Try different prompts to see how well your fine-tuned model performs.

OpenAI provides a playground to compare responses from multiple models.

✅️ Step 7: Evaluate and Deploy

Check if the model performs as expected.

Refine Data: If needed, add more examples to your dataset for better results.

Once you're satisfied with results,

Deploy this model in your client's application. Write a well structured system prompt.

Charge $15k-$20k for your fine-tuning job. (If the data is not structured and you had to work hard on the dataset, add extra cost)

Tip: First, try to train GPT-4o-mini as for most cases, this model works perfectly.

It is cheaper to train and cheaper to run as compared to GPT-4o.

That's it for today.

I hope you like this guide.

Bookmarked from Ole Lehmann @itsolelehmann

EU entrepreneurs are tired.

I analyzed the 190 responses to my post about the major problems European entrepreneurs suffer.

Here are the results of what frustrates them the most (I let Claude rank them)

The clear winner in terms of frustration is bureaucracy and paperwork (9/10).

People are fed up with all the forms, physical visits, and ridiculous notary requirements.

It came up again and again in different contexts.

Tax system and GDPR tie for second place (both 7/10).

The VAT system gives everyone headaches, especially when operating across borders (ask me about it!).

GDPR is interesting because while people generally support data protection, they feel the implementation crushes smaller businesses.

It's especially bad for small new businesses that can get crushed by the fines (there are law partner firms specialized only in finding and fining them, disgusting).

Labor laws and what people called an "anti-innovation culture" were next (both 6/10).

Tbh, this is close to my heart and impacts me daily.

The employment regulations seem to be a real pain point - lots of stories about not being able to fire underperforming employees and being afraid to hire because of the risks.

Market fragmentation and cross-border complexity (both 5/10) are interesting because they highlight a paradox:

The EU is supposed to be a single market, but entrepreneurs are still dealing with 27 different systems.

AI Act and tech regulations came up less frequently (4/10) but when they did, people felt strongly about them.

Some fascinating patterns emerged:

There's this striking cultural contrast people kept drawing between the US and EU approaches.

The US "let's do it" vs EU "let's regulate it" mentality came up several times.

The size issue is really interesting - lots of people pointed out how regulations that might be manageable for big companies are crushing for startups. One person mentioned spending up to 50% of their income just on compliance.

What really stood out to me:

Beyond specific complaints about regulations, there seems to be this deeper frustration about what some see as a fundamental hostility to entrepreneurship in the EU culture.

This all paints a picture of a challenge that goes beyond just regulatory burden - it seems to be about the whole culture around entrepreneurship in the EU.

Let me know if you find posts like this interesting.

I just analyzed it for myself and thought, why not share it.

I need only one simple example to convince you that OOP patterns are scams, and Category Theory patterns are superior.

In the OOP world, when you tell me something is a 'Visitor', you tell me nothing.

So are you telling me that this code traverses some values and maybe even recursively calls itself? Well, f*cking thanks, Sherlock, you just literally described 90% of every OCaml project.

Your words said nothing of value and wasted my time.

However, when you tell me that something is 'Applicative', I immediately know:

1. THE EXACT NAMES OF FUNCTIONS TO USE. Yes, the abstraction is THAT precise. It's and+/pure in OCaml, and the Star Wars TIE Interceptor ship operator/pure in Haskell.

2. THE EXACT TYPES OF EVERY FUNCTION.

3. How to create values of that thing: use 'pure'.

4. How to compose values of that thing: use 'and+'.

5. The parallelism semantics attached to the composition.

6. What extra rules and laws those functions follow.

7. An entire library of 100 REUSABLE FUNCTIONS I can immediately benefit from with my value.

8. What other abstractions are implied: every Applicative is Functor, so by definition, I know what else I can use with my value.

You said just one word — Applicative — and it singlehandedly absolutely f*cking destroyed entropy. I immediately know everything I need to know.

A single word says more than an entire useless book.
