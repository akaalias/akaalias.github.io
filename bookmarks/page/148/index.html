Bookmarked from Eric S. Raymond @esrtweet

Every war is a conflict between social systems of information processing. Which is why the Ukrainian invasion of Kursk Oblast has had a shattering effect well beyond what could be predicted from the troop numbers.

War is a brutally physical phenomenon. But the war machines and the factories that produce them have to be run by humans organized in social information systems.

Today Russia is reeling because in Kursk Oblast the Ukrainians are exploiting well-known weaknesses in Russia's system of social information processing.

Authoritarian systems need order, rigidity, and the programmed response to maintain themselves. The mental habits this encourages do not handle surprise and out-of-context problems well. So the first major achievement of the offensive - operational and strategic-level surprise - was exactly what the Ukrainians needed to do put the Russians in a situation where their mental habits hinder any effective response.

The consequences are very visible. Five days in, the Russians still do not have an operationally coherent counter. They're throwing scratch formations of conscripts at the Ukrainians with poor coordination, inviting the Ukrainians to defeat them piecemeal.

Even once the surprise wears off, authoritarian systems suffer from a slow OODA (observe, orient decide, act) loop. Orders have to be passed up and down the hierarchy, going through more steps that are required when initiative is delegated down to lower levels. This produces lag.

To worsen the lag, the Ukrainians need to make the information environment as confusing and ambiguous as possible. An important way to do this is to multiply threat axes. Which is why pushing troops over the border of Belgorod Oblast is an important move.

The Ukrainians will develop more threat axes. Some will be real. Some will be feints. All of them together will constitute a massive pysop designed to jam and overwhelm Russian information processing.

The most important failure of the Ukrainians need to avoid is getting bogged back down into a kind of warfare that is relatively static, where the challenges are predictable and thus well aligned to the authoritarian mode of social information processing.
