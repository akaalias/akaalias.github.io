Bookmarked from Kevin Yun @kevinyun

I just watched a video that was mind blowing. Making this one small adjustment in life can change your entire trajectory.

Here’s the concept:

In order to win, you need to have wins. In Winners keep winning. Losers keep losing. It’s a vicious cycle. That’s nature.

There was a study where scientists they made two mice fight each other. They rigged the first match, and the mouse that won the fight continued on to keep winning all the fights thereafter.

Another example is Mr Beast. Not a lot of people know that his first-ever video actually went viral. That sole event might have been all the difference in making him the biggest YouTuber in the world. Craving that vitality again, he wouldn’t stop hunting for it until he could repeat it over and over.

I’ve realized personally as well how much lacking that winning mentality has affected my output during periods of trough and sorrow. Being an entrepreneur, there’s so many days where it feels hopeless and redundant. It feels like you’re just going in circles and wasting time and energy. Most of the time, the day to day feels mundane and you question if what you’re doing is worth it.

Looking back on my lowest periods, I realize I was mentally in the opposite place of where I should have been. I needed to switch gears and have a winning mentality.

If you’re in a slump, all you can feel is down in the dumps. It’s so important you escape self pity and doubt, and get out of this rut as soon as possible.

But how? Not everyone can be an outright winner.

The key is to trick your brain into defining what is a win.

Start with the small wins. Get those dopamine hits.

Get out of bed today? That’s a win.
Get 2 hours of work done today? Thats a win.

Build it up slowly, but don’t ever regress to the pits of sorrow. Look at where you are right now relative to how far you’ve come.

The Passport King: How a Swiss Lawyer Built a Billion Dollar Passport Business

This Swiss Lawyer built a multibillion-dollar firm that helps wealthy individuals acquire passports and launched a fund that has returned 2000% since its inception.

He discovered a chance on a modest Caribbean island named St. Kitts & Nevis. The nation was grappling with significant crime and scant development until Chris Kälin introduced his brilliant idea:

A passport package that lets wealthy individuals buy citizenship for $250,000 without ever visiting the island

The passports provided access to 132 countries and exempted holders from capital gains taxes.

This citizenship program produced hundreds of millions in revenue from nothing for the island.

Chris Kälin`s company earned a 10% commission on each transaction, and he later duplicated this model in other nations, such as Grenada.

Kälin turned the pay for passport industry into a multibillion dollar market. In 2014, over $2B of them were bought.

Bookmarked from Boring_Business @BoringBiz_

Thoma Bravo is one of the best software PE firms in the world at $183BN in AUM and 75 portfolio companies that generated $27BN of revenues in 2023

Their Founder and Managing Partner, Orlando Bravo, was recently interviewed by David Rubenstein on software PE

Recommend giving a listen. Will leave a link to the video in comments

Some of my takeaways from the conversation:

Enterprise software has 3 key components that is investable from a private equity standpoint today: (i) applications (ii) infrastructure and (iii) cybersecurity

When Orlando started his career pre dot com burst, the deals he did were not big winners. He focused on IT services businesses that had a project based model

He did a 180 degree switch and moved his focus to recurring revenue businesses where he kept existing management in place

His advice to young people: "dont try to do something just a little bit better if it has not worked previously"

After subsequent successes, Thoma Bravo established itself as a software only firm in 2008 during the GFC

Today, Thoma Bravo's sweet spot for investments are companies that make between $500M - $1BN in revenue, growing 10-20% per year with recurring business model

His biggest advice to young people: learn communication skills. Private equity is all about selling effectively (convincing a company to take your capital or investors to give you their capital)

Best investment advice that Orlando Bravo ever received: minimize risk based on information you know

Not focusing on the business is the biggest mistake that investors make. Diligence, consulting reports, and quality of earnings are there for checking your work. Focusing on the 2-3 drivers of the business is the biggest advantage as a young investor
