Every war is a conflict between social systems of information processing. Which is why the Ukrainian invasion of Kursk Oblast has had a shattering effect well beyond what could be predicted from the troop numbers.

War is a brutally physical phenomenon. But the war machines and the factories that produce them have to be run by humans organized in social information systems.

Today Russia is reeling because in Kursk Oblast the Ukrainians are exploiting well-known weaknesses in Russia's system of social information processing.

Authoritarian systems need order, rigidity, and the programmed response to maintain themselves. The mental habits this encourages do not handle surprise and out-of-context problems well. So the first major achievement of the offensive - operational and strategic-level surprise - was exactly what the Ukrainians needed to do put the Russians in a situation where their mental habits hinder any effective response.

To worsen the lag, the Ukrainians need to make the information environment as confusing and ambiguous as possible. An important way to do this is to multiply threat axes. Which is why pushing troops over the border of Belgorod Oblast is an important move.

The Ukrainians will develop more threat axes. Some will be real. Some will be feints. All of them together will constitute a massive pysop designed to jam and overwhelm Russian information processing.

The most important failure of the Ukrainians need to avoid is getting bogged back down into a kind of warfare that is relatively static, where the challenges are predictable and thus well aligned to the authoritarian mode of social information processing.

I use service objects in all of my Rails projects.

👷‍♂️ This is how I engineer them:

1. Define the ApplicationService class with the call class method that does nothing but accepts any params and initializes a service, then calls the call instance method immediately

2. Define the initialize instance method that does nothing but accepts any params

3. Define a service that inherits from ApplicationService and defines the call method. If a service requires params, I define the initialize method, call super, then use the params. If you don't need params, then you don't need to define initialize at all

4. Implement the call method.

That's all!

🕓Ambient Agents

One of the UX patterns I'm most interested in is the idea of "ambient agents" working in the background for us

This can be super powerful... but needs some UX tricks to get right. So I wrote about it!

blog.langchain.dev/ux-for-agents-…

What are some considerations here?

👪 Human on the loop

We mostly talk about human-in-the-loop, but that doesn't work for ambient agents. Still - having observability and potentially control over what the agent does is important

So rather than have the human in the loop, what if they were on the loop? You can observe all that is happening, but after the fact.

Helps to build trust!

🤝Asking for help

Often times agents can't be fully autonomous. They may need input from a human on a particular point.

For example - I have an email agent which often needs input from me on how to fix this bug, whether I want to schedule this meeting, etc

This means that agents need a way to reach out to humans and ask for help. What might this look like? I think something like a customer support dashboard is a reasonable UX

If so - I'd love to talk! Please reach out or lmk what you think
