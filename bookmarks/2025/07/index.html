Bookmarked from Rohan Paul @rohanpaul_ai

The paper shows inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) lets LLMs learn their own reward signal, so they align better and think deeper.

Most teams still push imitation fine‑tuning and preference PPO, and that path stalls because no shared reward lives underneath.

IRL flips the script.

It watches expert traces or pairwise votes, then learns a neural function that scores any output.

With that score the model can search, plan, or self‑train without new human labels each step.

Math problems highlight the upside.

A simple right‑wrong reward lets agents grow long chains of thought and backtrack until answers lock in.

Chat models gain too, because pair choices are far cheaper than full demonstrations.

The authors map classic RL tools onto this setting and compare routes like PPO, DPO, Monte Carlo policy gradients, and Best‑of‑N filtering.

They warn of reward hacking, show ensemble or generative checks to spot it, and stress fresh data over sheer volume.

The bottom line is clear.

Learn the reward, not just the policy, and the model stays on target longer.

----

Paper – arxiv. org/abs/2507.13158

Paper Title: "Inverse Reinforcement Learning Meets LLM Post-Training: Basics, Advances, and Opportunities"

In vitro, psilocin (psilocybin’s metabolite) extended cellular lifespan by 29% at baseline and 57% at higher doses. It also reduced markers of aging like β-gal activity, boosted sirtuin (SIRT1) levels, lowered DNA damage and oxidative stress, and preserved telomeres.

Although psilocybin is listed as a Schedule I substance, these findings position it as a novel therapy for healthy aging and open a path toward 'psychedelic-assisted senotherapeutics', interventions that may enhance psychological well-being while also slowing genetic aging.
